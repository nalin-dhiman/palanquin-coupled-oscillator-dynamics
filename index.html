How to read this simulation

Walking moves the supports. Each carrier supplies two shoulder heights. A stride contains two steps: vertical bounce repeats twice per stride, while torso sway repeats once. Cadence and front/rear stride phase are prescribed inputs; the body’s heave, roll, pitch and contact loads are solved responses.

Try the comparison. Free pitch lets the load tilt forwards and backwards. Locked pitch keeps four contacts but prohibits that tilt. Two resultants average the front/rear inputs before the contact law; individual shoulder loads are then unresolved. “Hidden loads” shows how individual forces can change despite little visible rocking.

Live means new calculation. Physics advances with a fixed integration step in a worker, independently of the drawing frame rate. Pause freezes playback and stops requesting more physics. Edits restart from the initial state. Calculate full run computes the entire chosen duration for immediate seeking and a full export.

Display controls do not change the results. Camera, speed and motion magnification affect presentation only. The current numerical readouts, graphs, statistics and CSV always use actual values. Magnified views are labelled; force arrows in the contact view use 0.75 mm per newton.

These are assumed inputs. The rath’s dimensions, inertia, compliance and shoulder waveforms have not been measured. The model has no adaptive gait, foot–ground dynamics, forward travel, horizontal forces, yaw, pole bending or learned anticipation. A forward/backward tilt is not a calculation of where the procession goes.

Contact opening needs care. A compression-only spring and damper can produce a force jump at recontact. Peak load and zero-force statistics sample every integration step in the walking model; displayed traces use the output interval. Smaller steps help assess sensitivity, but the work–energy residual is not validation against people. The original model retains its earlier sampled-statistics definition.
